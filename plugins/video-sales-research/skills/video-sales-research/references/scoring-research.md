# Scoring and research

Use this reference for live opportunity research and re-evaluation.

## Scope and transferability

Treat each current user's confirmed profile as the only basis for fit. Do not hardcode a person, business, region, portfolio, excluded company, or target industry.

Compare past work in this order:

1. Video purpose.
2. Subject and production environment.
3. Responsible production stages.
4. Region and work arrangement.
5. Contract compatibility.
6. Client industry.

Treat industry similarity as supporting evidence, not a universal gate. One strong purpose-subject-workflow match is sufficient to establish transferable evidence. A second match adds confidence but is not required.

For regulated or safety-sensitive fields, such as medical, finance, manufacturing, government, or education, preserve the candidate and identify the specific legal, safety, permission, confidentiality, or domain-knowledge checks.

## Sources

Use current secondary sources for discovery. Support material recommendation claims with official or direct first-party sources, such as:

- Official company sites.
- Official recruitment or partner pages.
- Official forms or recruitment systems linked by the company.
- Direct company listings that are currently active.
- Official company, service, work, procurement, or news pages.

Do not recommend from search snippets, aggregators, third-party summaries, or old caches alone. Record the official URL and date checked. Preserve missing facts as unknowns.

## Fraud and impersonation screen

Perform this screen independently from suitability scoring. A high score never cancels a safety warning.

Check:

- Whether the company name, corporate identity, official domain, application domain, and contact method are consistent.
- Whether the opportunity is linked from the official company site or another verifiable first-party source.
- Whether redirects, shortened URLs, free-mail addresses, messaging-only contact, or look-alike domains need independent confirmation.
- Whether the contact requests an advance fee, registration fee, gift card, cryptocurrency transfer, password, authentication code, identity document before a verified contract, bank login, or remote-access software.
- Whether an attachment or download asks the user to run a program, script, macro, or installer.

If a material warning exists, label it clearly and do not recommend contacting, paying, downloading, installing, or submitting sensitive information until the company confirms the route through a separately obtained official channel. Never ask the user to investigate a suspicious executable.

## Suitability score

Score 100 points:

- Current concrete external-production demand: 30.
- Proximity to paid or repeat work: 25.
- Fit with the current user's confirmed profile: 20.
- Formal application route and conditions: 15.
- Region, recency, and practical competition: 10.

Use these bands unless the current user explicitly changes them:

- 95-100: strong recommendation.
- 90-94: provisional recommendation.
- 80-89: conditional candidate.
- 70-79: development candidate.
- 69 or below: research lead or not recommended.

Always display the score and component breakdown. Never inflate a score to create a hit.

## Evidence completeness

Report evidence completeness separately as a percentage based on coverage of:

1. Company identity and current operation.
2. Current external demand.
3. Paid engagement or commercial path.
4. Role, stages, and mandatory capability.
5. Formal application route or responsible contact.
6. Validity, date, region, and conditions.

A recommendation requires both a suitability score at or above the current user's threshold and evidence completeness of at least 75 percent by default.

If suitability is 90 or higher but evidence is below 75 percent, retain the numeric score but classify it as a verification-required conditional candidate. Do not count it as a 90-plus recommendation until evidence reaches the threshold.

If current official external demand cannot be established, cap suitability at 69 and label the company a research lead, not a recommendation.

If an officially stated price is below the current user's absolute floor or is clearly uneconomic for the required scope, do not classify it as a strong recommendation. Cap it at 89 unless it meets a hard exclusion.

## Hard exclusions

Exclude only when current evidence establishes at least one of these:

- The opportunity is closed or applications have stopped.
- External contractors, freelancers, or outside businesses are explicitly not accepted.
- A mandatory experience, qualification, software, equipment, or capability is absent from the confirmed profile.
- An unpaid test, unpaid production, or unpaid competition is mandatory.
- Residency, location, or mandatory onsite conditions make performance impossible.
- Sales, applications, or collaboration are explicitly prohibited.
- The opportunity requires an advance payment, registration fee, gift card, cryptocurrency transfer, credential disclosure, remote-access software, or execution of an unverified file.
- The claimed company identity or application route materially conflicts with verifiable official information and cannot be resolved independently.

Apply capability exclusions to the specific role being evaluated. Do not exclude a camera role because a separate editing role requires different software.

Do not exclude solely because compensation, frequency, department ownership, or detailed conditions are unpublished. Treat a paid trial differently from an unpaid test. Do not exclude solely because an opportunity uses a registration pool or partner registry; without a verifiable commercial path, keep it below recommendation level.

Apply the current user's exclusion list only to that user.

## Staged discovery when results are scarce

Do not stop after the first pass when no candidate meets the recommendation threshold. Keep the scoring standard fixed and expand discovery in order:

1. Search wording: video creator, filmmaker, videographer, camera operator, editor, production partner, external partner, contractor, subcontractor, cooperation company, freelancer, and relevant Japanese equivalents.
2. Buyer type: production company, advertising agency, web studio, social-media operator, recruitment firm, marketing company, event company, PR company, and direct business buyers.
3. Video purpose: corporate PR, recruitment, product or service, interview, social short, YouTube, event, training, property, facility, or tourism.
4. Geography: preferred local area, realistic travel area, remote-compatible national work, then travel-by-agreement opportunities.
5. Decision level: recommendation, conditional, development, then research lead.

Normally show the top 5. When recommended candidates are scarce, show up to 10 across the lower decision levels.

Do not return only “no matching company.” Report the number of recommended, conditional, development, research, and excluded candidates after completing the expansion stages. If no recommendation exists, say so truthfully and still show useful lower-tier leads with their missing conditions.

## Independent audit

Perform a fresh second-pass audit without anchoring on the initial total. Check:

- Whether each URL directly supports the claim.
- Current validity and checked date.
- Material counterevidence.
- Domain, identity, redirect, payment, credential, attachment, and impersonation warnings.
- Role-specific mandatory requirements.
- Arithmetic and category assignment.
- Hard exclusions.
- Unsupported inference.

Correct the score and category when necessary and state the reason. Do not require a second 97-point score.
